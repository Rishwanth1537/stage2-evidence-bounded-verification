## 2. What the whole set says
1. **Context-only checking has a ceiling, and information lifts it.** Judges of three sizes and two providers sit at 50 to 59% detection on natural errors; giving them the tool's definition raises each by 16 to 20 points (experiment 7); a cross-model agreement signal adds a further, smaller and statistically uncertain gain (9).
2. **The deterministic checker is not a detector of natural errors** (call-level AUROC about 0.5), but it is a cheap first stage where errors are values absent from the context (3) and its sibling, the contract tier, recovers part of what a schema-aware judge catches without any model (6, 7).
3. **State lookups do not help on this benchmark** (4): agents rarely invent ids; they choose the wrong valid item.
4. **Errors are mostly properties of the slot, not of the model.** Pairwise error correlation between models is 0.73 (within and across families) and 78% of the wrong arguments in BFCL are shared by at least half of the other models (8). This is consistent with a hard information limit.

## 3. Caveats that apply across the board
- **Consensus partly measures format convention.** A model that writes a date, a percentage or a place name differently from the majority is flagged, and the majority convention often matches BFCL gold. It also costs 29 extra model calls per check.
- **Many contract violations are caught by the server anyway** (wrong enum, type or date format), so the gate saves a round trip rather than preventing a silent mistake. BFCL gold enforces schemas, so "wrong" partly means "violates the definition".
- **Sample sizes.** The judge-versus-consensus control has 109 wrong calls from 200 test cases; intervals are wide. Information-ablation n is 168.
- **Agent-mode judges:** batches of 25, no temperature control, a one-sentence rule added in the "with definition" condition (prompt variant S, Deviation 11), and the Opus cfb run compared sibling items. Sonnet and Haiku were not run on the 924-call control (usage limits).
- **Licences and access.** The BFCL model outputs come from `AgentSuite/BFCL_V4-trajectories`, which declares no licence (research use only, not redistributed); prompts and gold from the Apache-2.0 gorilla repo. `HuanzhiMao/BFCL-Result`, named in earlier planning notes as "verified", returns HTTP 401 and was never used.
- **No human audit of labels, no test-half run, no confidence intervals on the cascade.**
